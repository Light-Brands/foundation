# Privacy

- **Keep the build log in this repository, and share the system document only with the member's own workspace.** The build log never goes to a service, a chat, a server or another repository. The system document (`key/knowledge/system.md`) goes to the member's own workspace when the member connects it, as knowledge for that workspace, and nowhere else. Both stay free of customer details. Why: the log describes the business and the member decides who sees it, and the system document is written to be read by the member's own workspace.
- **Never write customer details, business records or secrets into the build log, the system document or anywhere in the Key.** That covers names of customers, customers' email addresses and phone numbers, amounts from real records, PINs, keys and tokens. The Key holds no customer email address or phone number. The business's own contact details in the context block are expected. Why: these files are shared with the member's tools and travel with the repository.
- **Let the privacy check enforce it.** `checkLogText` in `src/lib/key/formats.ts` fails `npm test` when a log holds an email address or a phone number, and `log:sync` runs the same check before it writes. Why: a rule that depends on memory fails on a busy day.
- **Keep business records in the database.** Contacts, cards, bookings and everything else a customer touches live in tables (see `data.md`), and never in `key/`. Why: records change constantly, and a database is where they are protected and backed up.
- **Describe a change by what it does.** Write "reminders go out 24 hours before each session". Leave the customer out of the entry. Why: the point of a log entry is the system, and a name adds risk without adding meaning.
- **Use invented names in examples and tests.** Why: example data in a repository is read by everyone who reads the repository.
