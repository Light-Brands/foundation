# Privacy

- **Keep the build log in this repository, and share the system document only with the member's own workspace.** The build log never goes to a service, a chat, a server or another repository. The system document (`key/knowledge/system.md`) goes to the member's own workspace when the member connects it, as knowledge for that workspace, and nowhere else. Both stay free of customer details. Why: the log describes the business and the member decides who sees it, and the system document is written to be read by the member's own workspace.
- **Never write customer details, business records or secrets into the build log, the system document or anywhere in the Key.** That covers names of customers, customers' email addresses and phone numbers, amounts from real records, PINs, keys and tokens. The Key holds no customer email address or phone number. The business's own contact details in the context block are expected. Why: these files are shared with the member's tools and travel with the repository.
- **Let the privacy check enforce it.** `checkLogText` in `src/lib/key/formats.ts` fails `npm test` when a log holds an email address or a phone number, and `log:sync` runs the same check before it writes. Why: a rule that depends on memory fails on a busy day.
- **Keep business records in the database.** Contacts, cards, bookings and everything else a customer touches live in tables (see `data.md`), and never in `key/`. Why: records change constantly, and a database is where they are protected and backed up.
- **Describe a change by what it does.** Write "reminders go out 24 hours before each session". Leave the customer out of the entry. Why: the point of a log entry is the system, and a name adds risk without adding meaning.
- **Use invented names in examples and tests.** Why: example data in a repository is read by everyone who reads the repository.
- **Read every email address with `parseEmail` in `src/lib/email.ts`, and refuse what it refuses.** Drafting, the opt-out check at send time, unsubscribing, recording an opt-out and forget all use it: one plain ASCII address, sent with its local part as typed, matched lowercased with a plus tag dropped. Why: a second reading could let a variant of an opted-out address through, or carry a header into a message.
- **Keep `FOUNDATION_SECRET` the same for the life of the Foundation, and at least 32 characters long.** Opt-outs are stored as an HMAC of the address under it, so a new value makes every earlier opt-out unmatchable, and with no value set, or one shorter than 32 characters (`readEnv` treats it as unset), no opt-out is recorded or checked and no customer email is sent. Why: a stored opt-out must not be guessable from a list of known addresses, and it must keep working.
- **Draft every customer email into the outbox with `draftEmail`, and never send one that is not approved.** A draft is approved only by deciding its card, a sender claims one approved row at a time once its `send_after` has passed, and a row stuck in sending becomes sent only when the provider confirms it, else it waits for the owner and is never resent on its own. A provider success that arrives after recovery marked the row unconfirmed is recorded as sent, so every `Sender` times out well under `STUCK_AFTER_MS`. A subject holds no control character. Every send checks the opt-out list by the keyed hash of the address under the one rule in `src/lib/email.ts`, for the row's kind: an opt-out is `marketing` (written by the unsubscribe link) or `all` (written when the owner records a stop, and by forget), a `marketing` one never stops transactional email, and a second opt-out only widens the first. Why: a customer gets each message once, only after the owner's yes, and never after asking to stop.
- **Fail closed on every opt-out path.** `optOut` in `src/lib/data/outbox.ts` reads back that a row for the address now covers the scope asked for and throws otherwise, so the unsubscribe page says done only when it is. An opt-out list that cannot be read refuses the draft and leaves the claimed email failed for the owner, never sent. With `FOUNDATION_SECRET` unset or under 32 characters, recording and checking both refuse. Why: a lost opt-out means mail to someone who asked us to stop.
- **Offer an email for sending by hand only when nothing else will send it.** `handSendFor` in `src/lib/data/hand-send.ts` gives no copy for an address that opted out of the row's kind, and none for an approved row while `emailSendingActive` in `src/lib/data/connections.ts` says the email-sending connection is connected. A failing connection does not drain, so Copy email stays available. A failed row always offers it. `markSentByHand` refuses the same rows. Why: a customer gets each message once, and never after asking to stop.
- **Draft marketing email with `kind: "marketing"`, and let the sender add its footer.** The sender appends the postal address from the profile and a link that unsubscribes that message's address, and sets `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`. A marketing email is sent only where that link works, on the live site: every sender passes `senderOptions()` from `src/lib/data/outbox.ts`, whose address is `FOUNDATION_URL` when set (https only, else nothing) and otherwise the host's production address, and without one a marketing email stays approved and unsent. The opt-out list is checked again at send time. Why: every person who gets marketing email can stop it in one step, and it stops.
