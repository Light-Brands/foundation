# Copy

These rules cover every screen, prompt, document and message the project writes.

- **Write US English.** Use "color", "organize" and "center". Why: one spelling across every surface reads as one voice.
- **Use commas, colons and periods, and no long or short dash characters.** Write a hyphen only inside a word or a slug. A test fails on both dash characters in the standards and the prompts. Why: it keeps the writing plain and the same everywhere.
- **State what a thing is.** Write "Booking sends reminders", and never describe a thing by what it is not before saying what it is. The one exception is when the absence is the point, such as "no fee". Why: the reader should never have to hold a wrong idea before getting the right one.
- **Use plain words a business owner uses.** Say "set up", "connect" and "what you have built". Never use a term a developer would need to look up without explaining it. Why: the owner reads this, not only the builder.
- **Never use an internal persona name in the repository.** A role's persona is chosen by the business and lives in its `key.yaml`. Why: names from Light Brands' own systems mean nothing to a member and tie the repository to them.
- **Never name the workspace platform the Key is shaped for.** A test (`tests/unit/no-platform-name.test.ts`) checks every tracked file. Why: members see one product, and the mapping lives in a private place.
- **Use "Request a seat" as the one join phrase.** Write it in sentence case, exactly. Why: one phrase for one action is easy to recognize and easy to search for.
- **Say so when a number is an estimate.** Write "about 2 to 4 hours a week" for a range you cannot promise. Why: an estimate that reads as a promise becomes a complaint.
- **Keep messages short and kind.** Say what happened, then what to do next, in one or two sentences. Why: a person who sees an error needs a next step more than an explanation.
- **Write headings in sentence case.** Why: it matches the rest of the interface.
